# AI Policy Explainer — Post Template

> Use this template for every post in the series. The goal is clarity and memorability — written for a general audience, useful for canvassers. No jargon. No big words. One idea at a time.

-----

## Post Metadata (fill in before publishing)

- **Series:** AI Policy for Everyone
- **Post #:** [X of X]
- **Topic:** [Name of the policy or tax being explained]
- **Policy Type:** [e.g. Tax / Regulation / Public Fund / Labor Protection]

-----

## TLDR

*One to three sentences. What is this policy? What does it do? Who does it affect?*

> Write this like you’re explaining it to a neighbor at the door. No acronyms. No policy-speak.

-----

## The Analogy

*Find an everyday comparison that makes the policy click immediately.*

Questions to answer here:

- What does this policy remind you of in real life?
- What would it feel like if this policy existed in a non-tech context?
- What’s the simplest possible version of this idea?

> Tip: The best analogies involve things people already pay for, own, or interact with — like factories, landlords, utilities, or paychecks.

-----

## This Isn’t New — We Just Never Got It Right

*Find the historical parallel. Show that we’ve faced a version of this problem before.*

Questions to answer here:

- Has technology ever displaced workers like this before?
- Was there a policy response? Did it work?
- What happened when we *didn’t* act?

> Tip: Frame it as unfinished business, not ancient history. We’ve been here before. We got it wrong. Here’s why we have another shot.

-----

## The Existing Policy Closest to This

*Find a real, current policy that is either similar to what’s being proposed — or doing the opposite.*

Questions to answer here:

- Is there already a law or tax that does something like this?
- Is there a policy that actually makes the problem *worse*?
- What country or state has tried something like this?

> Tip: South Korea’s 2017 reduction in automation tax incentives and the U.S. Section 179 equipment deduction are good reference points for automation-related posts.

-----

## What Would Actually Be Taxed (or Regulated)?

*Get specific. What exactly does this policy target?*

Questions to answer here:

- What is the thing being taxed, regulated, or funded?
- How would it be measured or calculated?
- Is there anything that’s *not* included that people might assume is?

> Tip: Be honest about the grey areas and unanswered questions. That’s what makes the next post.

-----

## Who Would Be Affected?

*Break it into who benefits and who pays.*

- **Who pays or is regulated?**
- **Who benefits?**
- **Who’s in the grey zone?** (This is often the most interesting part)

> Tip: Avoid “rich vs. poor” framing — get specific about which *types* of companies, workers, or industries are in scope.

-----

## Where Would the Money Go? (or What Does the Policy Do?)

*For tax proposals: where does the revenue go? For regulations: what changes in practice?*

Questions to answer here:

- What programs or funds would this support?
- Who administers it?
- Is this replacing something that already exists, or building something new?

-----

## Why This Matters Right Now

*Connect the policy to the current moment. What’s happening today that makes this urgent?*

- What data or trend makes this timely?
- What happens if we *don’t* act?
- Who is already being affected?

> Tip: Use concrete, recent numbers where possible. Avoid vague statements like “many people are affected.”

-----

## The Bottom Line (For the Doorstep)

*This is the section canvassers memorize. Two to four sentences, max. Plain language only.*

> Write it in quotes, like you’re handing someone a script.

*Example format:*

> *”[What the policy is in one sentence]. [Why it matters in one sentence]. [What happens if we don’t act in one sentence]. [What we’re asking for in one sentence].”*
