# What Is an AI Profits Tax — and Why Does It Matter?

> **Series:** AI Policy for Everyone | **Post:** 1 of [X]  
> **Topic:** Taxing the Gains from Automated Labor

-----

## TLDR

When a company uses AI to do work that people used to do, and makes more money because of it, an AI profits tax would make that company pay taxes on those gains — and use that money to support the workers and communities that got left behind.

-----

## The Analogy

Think about a factory owner who replaces their whole assembly line with robots. The owner still makes money — probably more money than before. But the workers who built those products? They’re gone. And the payroll taxes they used to pay into Social Security, Medicaid, and other safety net programs? Gone too.

An AI profits tax says: if your machines are doing the work, you should help pay for the benefits that work used to fund.

-----

## This Isn’t New — We Just Never Got It Right

This isn’t the first time technology has displaced workers on a massive scale.

In the early 1800s in England, skilled textile workers called the **Luddites** watched as factory machines replaced their trades overnight. They didn’t just smash machines out of anger — they actually tried to *negotiate*. They asked factory owners for a textile tax to support worker pensions and a phased introduction of new machines. They were ignored.

The productivity gains from the Industrial Revolution made factory owners enormously wealthy. Workers bore the cost — through job loss, wage collapse, and poverty — while the people who owned the machines kept the profits.

We’ve been here before. And we never built the policy to make it fair.

-----

## The Existing Policy Closest to This

Right now, the U.S. tax code actually does the *opposite* of an AI profits tax. Through deductions like **Section 179**, companies can immediately write off the full cost of buying automation equipment. In other words, the government currently *rewards* companies for replacing workers with machines.

An AI profits tax would start to rebalance that — not by punishing technology, but by making sure the economic gains from automation are shared more broadly.

-----

## What Would Actually Be Taxed?

Under OpenAI’s proposal, the focus is on **shifting where tax revenue comes from** — away from wages and payroll taxes, and toward:

- **Capital gains** — the profits companies make from investments and AI-driven growth
- **Corporate income** — taxing the companies that profit most from automation
- **Automated labor taxes** — a levy roughly equivalent to the payroll taxes a displaced worker would have generated

So it’s not a blanket tax on owning AI. It’s a tax on the *profits* that come from using AI to replace human work.

-----

## Who Would Be Affected?

- **Large corporations** that use AI to automate jobs at scale (think logistics, customer service, white-collar office work)
- **Tech companies** whose products are built on AI-driven labor replacement
- **Investors and shareholders** who see capital gains rise as automation boosts corporate profits

What about a company like Anthropic, which uses AI to help build AI? That’s a genuinely tricky question — and one that policymakers will need to define carefully. The key question is: are you using AI to *replace workers*, or to build and improve your product?

-----

## Where Would the Money Go?

OpenAI’s proposal suggests the revenue could fund:

- **Social Security, Medicaid, SNAP, and housing assistance** — programs currently funded by payroll taxes that shrink as AI replaces workers
- **Worker retraining programs** — structured pathways into fields like healthcare, education, and elder care
- **A national public wealth fund** — so every citizen has a direct stake in AI-driven economic growth

-----

## Why This Matters Right Now

AI is already displacing white-collar workers. White-collar payrolls have contracted for 29 consecutive months — a stretch economists say is unprecedented outside a recession. If we don’t act, the wealth generated by AI will flow almost entirely to the people who own it, while everyone else absorbs the cost.

An AI profits tax is one way to make sure that doesn’t happen.

-----

## The Bottom Line (For the Doorstep)

> *“AI is making companies a lot of money by replacing workers. An AI profits tax says: if machines are doing the work, the people who own those machines should help pay for the safety nets that workers used to fund. We’ve been here before — during the Industrial Revolution — and we got it wrong. This time, we have a chance to get it right.”*
